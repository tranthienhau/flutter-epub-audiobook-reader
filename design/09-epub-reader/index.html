Chapter 7 · The Long Paddock

The Salt Road

VII. Across the Bitter Tide

The frost had settled early across the southern paddocks, turning the dry spear-grass into a brittle, shimmering crust beneath the mare's hooves. Meg rode with the reins slack between two gloved fingers, listening to the wire fence hum in the salt wind that swept up off the bight. In this light, between the setting of the winter moon and the first pale blush above the ridge, the boundary fence seemed less like a fence and more like a spine holding the timber country to the sea.

She knew every post by heart, the split red gum, the ironbark weathered to silver, the notch her father had cut the summer the drought finally broke. A magpie shifted upon the strainer wire, shaking a shower of rimed dew onto the damp clay, its warble quiet and reedy in the grey chill before dawn.

Far out on the darkening water, a single coastal steamer pressed eastward toward Melbourne, its masthead lantern blinking once, twice, before the sea mist swallowed it whole.
